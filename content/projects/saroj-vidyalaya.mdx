## The problem

There's a failure mode in explanatory writing where the explanation is
technically correct and completely useless. It defines each term with three more
terms. It's rigorous, and it only works for a reader who could have skipped it.

The alternative isn't dumbing anything down. It's finding the analogy that
carries the actual structure of the idea — the one that keeps working when the
reader pushes on it. That's difficult, slow, and mostly what teaching is.

## What I built

A platform organised around that principle rather than around a syllabus. It
describes itself as a digital garden rather than a curriculum, which is an
accurate description of how it grows: topics, courses and a section called New
Horizons, added as they're written properly rather than to fill a structure.

The editorial standard is the constraint that shapes the build — every design
decision is downstream of "does this help someone hold an unfamiliar idea?"

## Why it's here

It's a memorial as much as a project, built in memory of Saroj Singh, a teacher.
That's the reason the standard is what it is, and the reason it isn't finished
and won't be rushed.

Professionally, it's the clearest example of the editorial work I take on for
other people: turning something genuinely difficult into something a
non-specialist can hold, without lying about it in the process.
